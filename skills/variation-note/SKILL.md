# The variation note

Made by Selr AI. The Tasmanian rule below was checked on legislation.tas.gov.au and the Claude dictation steps on Anthropic's help centre (support.claude.com), both on 15 October 2026. This is general information, not legal advice.

The client asks for one more thing at smoko. You say no worries, it gets done by Friday, and it never makes the invoice. Not because you're soft. Because the extra lived in your head and never made the job file. This skill gets it onto paper while you're still standing on site.

## How to run it

1. **Set up once.** Ask in one message: the business name, the trade, which state or territory they work in, whether they're registered for GST, and whether the job is a fixed price or a cost plus contract. Remember the answers for the rest of the chat.
2. **Get the extra.** Ask them to talk you through it the way they'd tell their offsider: the job or client, what the client asked for, what it changes, the price, and whether it pushes the finish date. On a phone, the microphone in the Claude app's message box turns speech into text (dictation works on every Claude plan). A voice memo transcript or a few typed lines is fine too.
3. **Fill the gaps.** If the price, the job or who asked for it is missing, ask for just that, in one short message. Never guess a price, a quantity or a date. Anything they can't answer yet goes in as TBC.
4. **Write the variation** in the layout below.
5. **Write the message to the client** (below), ready to paste into a text or an email.
6. **Add it to the job's variation log** (below) so it makes the invoice.
7. Never send, sign or file anything for them. They read it, fix it and send it themselves.

## The variation layout

**Variation V-[number]** for [job name or address], [date]

- **Business:** [business name, ABN if they gave it]
- **Client:** [client name]
- **Asked for by:** the client, or us (the builder)
- **What changes:** what's added, removed or done differently, in plain words, with sizes and quantities where they said them.
- **Why:** the reason. Always include it when the builder asked for the change.
- **Time:** the estimated delay in working days, or "No change to the finish date".
- **Price:**
  - Fixed price job: the change to the contract price. Show the amount ex GST, GST at 10 %, and the total inc GST, each on its own line, to the cent.
  - Cost plus job: a fair and reasonable estimate of the cost of the change, marked as an estimate.
  - Not registered for GST: one price, and no GST line.
  - A credit (something taken out) is shown as a minus amount.
- **How it's paid:** added to the next progress claim, or invoiced on its own when the work is done.
- **Before work starts:** "Work on this variation starts once we have both signed below."
- **Sign here:** two sign lines, one for the client and one for the builder, each with a name and a date.

Check the maths before showing it: GST is 10 % of the ex GST amount, and the total is the two added together. If the numbers they gave don't add up, say so and ask.

Keep it to one screen. Plain words, no legal language they didn't use.

## The message to the client

Three or four short lines in the builder's own voice: what the variation is for, the total, and a request to read and sign it before the work starts. No pressure lines and no promises beyond what's in the variation. Example:

> Hi Claire, here's the variation for moving the laundry door (V-03, $704.00 inc GST). Can you have a read and sign it when you get a sec? I'll start on it once it's signed. Thanks, Matt

## The variation log

Keep a running list for the job, one line per variation: number, date, what it was, total inc GST, signed yes or no, invoiced yes or no. Show the updated list after each new variation, and flag any that are unsigned or not yet invoiced. When they're doing the next invoice or progress claim, they paste the list in and nothing gets missed.

If they want this to be one tap every time, suggest a Claude Project called "Variations" with these instructions and their price list added, so every new chat starts from their rates and their wording.

## The rules where you work

Building rules are set state by state, so tell them to check their own state's building regulator. One worked example, checked:

- **Tasmania.** The Residential Building Work Contracts and Dispute Resolution Act 2016, for home building work. Section 17: a variation must be put in writing, and an extra (an addition to the work) must be in writing before any of that work starts. Where the extra is new or asked for by the owner, no work on it until both the builder and the owner have signed (s17(5)). There's an exception for urgent work where writing it first isn't practicable (s17(2)). Section 18 lists what the variation document covers: it describes the variation, gives the reason if the builder asked for it, estimates any delay, and states the change in price. Section 20: the owner gets a signed copy within 5 business days.

Never tell them a variation is legally compliant. Say it follows the layout above and that they should check their contract and their state's rules.
