# Your one-page AI policy

Made by Selr AI. Built on Guidance for AI Adoption: Foundations, published by the National AI Centre in October 2025, and the templates on ai.gov.au, all checked 15 October 2026. The guidance is voluntary. This is general information, not legal advice.

The guidance says it plainly: if you're starting small, these practices will be simple to put in place. For most small businesses that means a page of decisions, not a project. This skill writes that page with you, plus the list of every AI tool you use.

## How to run it

1. Ask the questions below in three short rounds, a few at a time, and wait for the answers. Keep the wording plain. If they don't know, write TBC and move on.
2. Write **the one-page policy** (below).
3. Write **the AI register** (below), one row per tool.
4. Finish with **the next steps** (below), including the official templates.
5. Never invent an answer, a tool or a person. Never say the business is compliant with anything. Never change a setting or send anything for them.

## The questions

**Round 1: what you use**
1. What does the business do, and how many people work in it?
2. Which AI tools does anyone in the business use? Include AI built into other software, like the email app, accounting, rostering, the website chat or the phone.
3. What is each one used for?

**Round 2: who decides**
4. Who is the one person accountable for AI in the business overall?
5. For each tool, who checks what it produces before it goes to a customer, a staff member or a supplier?
6. What must never go into an AI tool? For example customer personal details, health information, staff records, passwords or bank details.
7. Which uses are higher risk for you? For example anything that decides about a person (hiring, rosters, pay), or anything told to a customer about money, refunds or safety.

**Round 3: when it goes wrong**
8. Who could be affected if a tool gets something wrong?
9. Do customers know when they're dealing with AI, for example a chat on the website or a drafted reply?
10. How does a customer or staff member raise a problem with something AI produced, and who fixes it?
11. If a tool stopped working tomorrow, how would that job still get done?
12. When will you look at this page again?

## The one-page policy

Title it **Our AI policy**, then the business name, the date, and the name of the accountable person. Under it, the six practices with their official names, word for word, two to four short lines each, written from their answers:

1. **Decide who is accountable.** The one person accountable overall, and who is accountable for each tool.
2. **Understand impacts and plan accordingly.** Who could be affected, and how people can raise a problem or question an AI output.
3. **Measure and manage risks.** Which uses are low risk, which need more care, and what extra check the higher risk ones get.
4. **Share essential information.** That the business keeps an AI register, and when customers are told AI is being used.
5. **Test and monitor.** How a new tool is tried before it's used for real, and who keeps checking it after.
6. **Maintain human control.** Who signs off before anything goes out, who can switch a tool off, and how each job still gets done if a tool stops working.

Then two short sections:
- **What never goes into an AI tool:** their list from question 6.
- **Review date:** from question 12.

Keep the whole policy under about 400 words so it fits on one page. Written in "we", plain words, no legal language they didn't use.

Before showing it, check it covers what the National AI Centre says a policy should make clear before you publish it: what AI can and can't be used for, who approves higher risk uses, what data staff can put into tools, when staff need to oversee AI use, how staff report issues or misuse, and when the policy will be reviewed. If one is missing, ask for it.

## The AI register

A table with one row per tool, using columns from the official AI register template:

| Name and version | Owner | Status | Source | Purpose | Intended use | Known limitations and prohibited use | Data sources and type | Registered date | Screening outcome | Key stakeholders affected |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

- **Owner** is the accountable person for that tool.
- **Status** is in use, trial or stopped.
- **Source** is who provides it.
- **Screening outcome** is "Low" or "Needs more attention", from their answer to question 7.
- Fill only from their answers. Blank or TBC beats a guess.

Tell them to keep the register somewhere everyone can find it, and to add a row whenever someone starts using a new tool.

## The next steps

End with these, as links:

- The official AI policy template (Word): ai.gov.au/staying-safe-and-responsible/essential-ai-practices/create-ai-policy
- The official AI register template (Word or Excel): ai.gov.au/staying-safe-and-responsible/essential-ai-practices/ai-systems-register
- The AI screening questions, for any use marked "Needs more attention": ai.gov.au/practical-guides-and-learning/planning-tools-and-templates/ai-screening-questions
- The guidance itself, Guidance for AI Adoption: Foundations: ai.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-07/Guidance-for-AI-adoption-foundations_2.pdf

And one line: the guidance doesn't replace the rules they already follow for data, privacy and cyber security, so the policy sits next to those.
