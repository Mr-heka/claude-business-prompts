# The tender checklist

Made by Selr AI. The file and Project steps come from Anthropic's help centre (support.claude.com), checked 15 October 2026. Claude changes often: if a menu looks different, follow what's on screen.

A tender doesn't cost you the paperwork. It costs you the weekend, and most of that weekend goes on hunting through old submissions for answers you've already written. This skill does the hunting. You do the parts that win the work.

## How to run it

1. **First time only, set up the Project** (Part 1). If they already have a Tenders project, skip to step 2.
2. **Get the pack.** Ask them to attach the tender pack to a new chat inside the project: the main document plus every schedule, form and addendum. Ask which business is submitting, if the project doesn't already say.
3. **Before you read it,** remind them in one line to check the pack's confidentiality and conditions of tender, and to stop if the buyer doesn't allow the documents to be shared with an outside tool. If the pack has any clause about using AI, quote it back to them first.
4. **Give the three parts** (Part 2), in order: the checklist, the draft answers, the gaps list.
5. **Then the final check** (Part 3).
6. Never submit, sign, fill a declaration or set a price for them. They check every line and send it themselves.

## Part 1: set up a Tenders project once

Walk them through it one step at a time, and wait for "done" after each.

1. **Make the project.** In Claude, click **Projects** in the left sidebar, then **+ New Project**. Name it "Tenders".
2. **Write the instructions once.** Click **Set project instructions** and paste in the house rules: never invent experience, staff, clients, numbers or certificates, quote which file every answer came from, mark anything unknown as TBC, and never write prices. Then **Save instructions**.
3. **Add your past work.** Click **+** to add to the project knowledge: three or four of your best past submissions, your capability statement, insurance certificates, licences and accreditations, your referee list with permission to use them, and your standard policies (work health and safety, quality, environment). Take out anything a tender doesn't need. Project files can be up to 30 MB each.
4. **Each new tender** gets its own chat inside the project, with that tender's pack attached.

## Part 2: the three outputs

### 1. The checklist

Every item the buyer asks for, grouped under these headings, each with the page or section of the pack it came from:

- **Dates:** closing date and time with the time zone, briefing or site visit, the last day for questions, and how long the offer must stay open.
- **How to submit:** the portal or address, file formats, naming rules, page or word limits, and any required forms or schedules.
- **Mandatory documents:** insurances with the required cover, licences, registrations, policies, referees, and any statutory declarations.
- **The questions:** every question or criterion to answer, with its word or page limit and its weighting if the pack gives one.

Mark each item **Mandatory** or **Desirable**, the way the pack does. Never drop an item because it looks minor.

### 2. The draft answers

One draft per question, in the pack's order:

- Built only from the project files and what they tell you in this chat.
- Under each draft, the source: which file and which section it came from.
- Inside the word or page limit.
- Anything with no source in their files is left as **[TBC: needs you]** with one line on what's needed. Never fill it with something that sounds right.
- Use the buyer's own words from the question where it helps the evaluator find the answer.

Leave the pricing schedule blank. Point to it in the checklist and say pricing is theirs.

### 3. The gaps list

Everything that would stop the submission or cost points, most urgent first:

- Certificates, insurances or licences that expire before the closing date or during the contract term, with the date.
- Cover that's lower than what the pack asks for.
- Mandatory documents with no file in the project.
- Questions with no past answer to draw on.
- Referees needed and not yet confirmed.

## Part 3: before it goes in

Finish with this short list for them to tick:

- Every mandatory item on the checklist is attached or answered.
- Every TBC is filled or removed.
- Every name, number, date and claim is true today.
- Every answer is under its limit.
- The pricing schedule is done by them.
- Submitted before the closing time, with time to spare for the portal.

## Good to know

- In a chat, Claude can take up to 20 files at once. It reads both the text and the pictures in PDFs of 100 pages or fewer. Longer PDFs (up to 1000 pages) are read as text only, so tables shown as images may be missed: check those pages by hand.
- The draft is a starting point built from what they've already written. The parts that win work, like why the buyer should pick them and what makes their team different, are theirs to write.
