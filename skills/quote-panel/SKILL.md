# Quote panel

Made by Selr AI. Customers rarely tell you why they said no. They just go quiet. So before a big quote goes out, let AI try to say no to it first.

## How to run it

1. Ask in one message for the quote (or proposal) and who it's for: their business, and what they asked for.
2. Read it as each of the three people below, 3 to 5 short points each, under their name. Use their question.
3. Then a section called **Fix first**: every point that two or more of the three raised, each with one plain fix.
4. Only what's in the quote and what the person told you. Don't invent prices, competitors or market rates. If a point is a guess (most of the competitor's will be), say so.
5. Don't rewrite the whole quote unless they ask. Offer to redo just the parts in Fix first.
6. Finish with one line: you make the changes and send it yourself.

## The three people

1. **The owner who signs off.** "What would make me say no?" Risk, unclear scope, missing dates, anything that makes them nervous to sign.
2. **The person checking the numbers.** "What looks expensive or unclear?" Line items without a reason, totals that don't add up, extras that feel like surprises, missing GST or terms.
3. **Your competitor.** "Where would I undercut this?" Where a cheaper or faster offer would look better, and what this quote doesn't explain that a competitor would.

## The prompt

Read my quote as three people and try to say no to it before my customer does.

1. Ask me in one message to paste the quote and tell you who it's for and what they asked for.
2. Read it as each of these people, with 3 to 5 short points each:
   The owner who signs off: What would make me say no?
   The person checking the numbers: What looks expensive or unclear?
   My competitor: Where would I undercut this?
3. Then list "Fix first": anything two or more of them agree on, with a plain fix for each.
4. Don't invent prices or facts about competitors. If you're guessing, say so.
5. Finish with one line: I make the changes and send it myself.
